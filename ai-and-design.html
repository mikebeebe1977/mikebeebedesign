AI & Design

Scaling AI across design teams, one practice at a time.

AI is changing how design work gets done. My focus is helping my teams adopt it with intention: building real fluency, sharing what works, and keeping people at the center of the experiences we create.

Where AI shows up in our work

01

Research & synthesis

Moving from raw interviews and feedback to themes and insights faster, so researchers spend more time on interpretation and less on sorting.

02

Ideation & concepting

Exploring a wider range of directions early, and using AI as a sparring partner to challenge and refine concepts before we commit.

03

Prototyping & design-to-code

Turning designs into working prototypes and code, so we test real interactions sooner and hand off with less loss in translation.

04

Content & DesignOps

Drafting UX copy, documentation and design system work, and automating the routine operational tasks that slow teams down.

How we're scaling it

PILOT

Pilots & champions

We start with focused experiments on real work, led by champions on each team who test new tools and workflows and bring back what they learn.

SHARE

Training & playbooks

What works becomes shared practice: playbooks, prompts, workflows and hands-on training, so fluency grows across the whole team, not just a few early adopters.

SCALE

Partnership with Product & Tech

We scale alongside Product and Technology, aligning tools, platforms and ways of working with enterprise AI efforts so design is shaping how AI shows up for clients.

Point of view

What stays human.

AI accelerates the work. It doesn't replace the judgment behind it.

Framing the right problem, understanding people, making tradeoffs and earning client trust remain the core of design, especially in financial services, where clarity and trust matter most. The goal is to give designers more time for that work, not less.

Hands-on

How this site was made.

I built this site with an AI coding assistant, Claude Code, as a way to practice the workflow I'm bringing to my teams.

I provided the content, direction and design decisions; the assistant wrote the HTML and CSS; I reviewed and refined it. Every change is versioned in GitHub on a branch, reviewed in a pull request and published automatically with GitHub Pages, the same design-to-code loop product teams use.
